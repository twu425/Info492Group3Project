# BidGrid

BidGrid is a Caltrans construction cost explorer. It lets you browse historical
highway construction contracts on a map, dig into how unit prices for specific
work items have moved over time and across regions, and build a preliminary bid
item estimate for a new location with help from an AI assistant.

The app is built with Next.js (App Router), React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS,
MapLibre GL for the map, and the Vercel AI SDK with Anthropic's Claude for the
estimating assistant.

## What you can do

- **Explore contracts on a map.** Every contract is a marker colored by its work
  category. Filter by district, bid year, and item family.
- **Study an item family.** Open any family to see a unit price trend over time
  (quarterly weighted average with a P25 to P75 band), per unit statistics, a map
  of where the work was bid, and a sortable table of individual line item bids.
  The table shows how each bid ranked on its item and on the overall contract,
  which quantity band it falls in, and how its price compares to what is typical
  for that quantity.
- **Inspect a contract or a bidder.** Drill into a single contract's bidders and
  line items, or into a bidder's history across contracts.
- **Build an estimate.** Drop a pin anywhere, describe the work in plain language,
  and the assistant maps it to catalog items, pulls comparable historical prices
  near the pin, and assembles a bid item list with quantities and prices you can
  edit. Estimates can be saved as drafts in the browser.

## The data is synthetic

BidGrid does not use real Caltrans bid data. Everything you see is generated by a
deterministic model in `lib/bidgrid/data.ts`. The goal is a dataset that behaves
like real highway bid tabs (plausible prices, realistic escalation, regional cost
differences, and competitive bidding spread) while being fully self contained,
reproducible, and free of any real procurement records.

A seeded pseudo random generator drives the whole model, so the dataset is
identical on every run. The current build contains:

- 233 contracts across 6 Caltrans districts (2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 11), bid between 2016
  and 2026.
- 76 item codes grouped into 26 item families and 7 categories.
- 30 fictional contractor profiles.

### How a contract is generated

1. **Project type.** Each contract is assigned a weighted project type (pavement
   rehab, interchange reconstruction, bridge widening, safety or barrier upgrade,
   drainage repair, ADA curb ramps, signal or lighting upgrade, soundwall, or
   landscaping). Some types are weighted differently by district, so rural
   districts lean toward pavement and drainage while urban districts see more
   interchanges and soundwalls.
2. **Location and size.** The contract lands in a county within the district, on a
   real route number for that county, with a project size drawn log uniformly
   from the type's typical range.
3. **Items and quantities.** Items are chosen from the catalog based on the project
   type's core and optional families. Quantities are scaled to hit the target
   project size, with mild random variation.
4. **Engineer's price per item.** A base 2016 unit price for each item is adjusted
   by a time escalation index, a district cost factor, and a small quantity
   elasticity (larger quantities bid slightly cheaper per unit). Haul distance
   pushes some remote (District 2) prices above the cities.
5. **Bidders.** A set of contractors is selected by home district, specialty match,
   and firm size relative to the job. Each contractor carries a pricing behavior
   (low, mid, high, or erratic) that shapes how aggressively it bids.
6. **Bids.** Every selected contractor bids every line item, applying its behavior,
   per line noise, and the occasional unbalanced line. Bids are then ranked by
   total.

### Price realism

- **Escalation.** Unit prices rise about 3 to 5 percent per year, with a single
  larger jump of roughly 10 to 15 percent spread across 2021 and 2022 to mimic the
  material cost spike of that period. Materials heavy work sits at the top of that
  band. A typical item's one year change lands roughly between negative 5 percent
  and positive 10 percent.
- **Bid spread.** Losing bids usually come in 2 to 25 percent above the low bid,
  occasionally up to 40 percent, and never past 60 percent.
- **Lump sum items.** Lump sum items (such as clearing and grubbing or a lighting
  system) are priced only from projects of a similar total size, so a small job
  does not inherit a large interchange's lump sum figure.
- **Comparable coverage.** The generator tops up the dataset near the Bay Area and
  Los Angeles metros so that every item code has at least 8 recent comparables
  within 50 miles there. Estimates in those regions never fall back to just a
  couple of data points.

### Derived analytics

On top of the raw contracts, `lib/bidgrid/estimate.ts` and `lib/bidgrid/family.ts`
compute the numbers the UI shows:

- **Escalation factor** per item and quarter, used to restate old prices in current
  dollars so different years are comparable.
- **Comparables** for a pin: line items for an item code within 50 miles and the
  last 5 years, from the top three bidders, with prices escalated to now.
- **Quantity bands** (Small, Medium, Large) from the 33rd and 67th percentiles of
  all of an item code's quantities.
- **Vs. typical** price, from a log log regression of escalated price on quantity,
  which flags bids that are unusually high or low for their size.

The same band and vs. typical logic is used in both the item family table and the
estimate's comparable rows, so the two views always agree.

## Running locally

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

The estimating assistant calls Anthropic's API. Set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in a
`.env.local` file at the project root before using the build estimate feature.

```
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=your_key_here
```

## Project layout

- `app/` Next.js routes, including the `api/build` endpoint for the assistant.
- `components/bidgrid/` the explorer, map, filters, detail views, and build drawer.
- `lib/bidgrid/data.ts` the synthetic data model.
- `lib/bidgrid/estimate.ts` pricing, comparables, bands, and the price curve.
- `lib/bidgrid/family.ts` item family statistics and trend series.
- `lib/bidgrid/build-tools.ts` the tool definitions the assistant uses.
