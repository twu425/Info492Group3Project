Team 3 — Bidding and cost planning
15 peer reviews, the most of any team. Your stakeholder interview was the single most-praised element in the class this week — four reviewers named it as their favorite part, and their reasons were consistent: it showed what bidding actually looks like for a real person, and then showed how your agent addresses the problems that person described. Nobody else grounded their work in a practitioner's words. The demo flow also landed hard, with reviewers citing the map view, the price trends, and the path from map to item detail to draft as evidence of strong underlying logic.
Two things cost you. The first isn't your fault but did show up in the data: because you didn't present Demo #1, the "what changed" question was unanswerable, and three reviewers answered "couldn't tell" while two wrote explicitly that they had no Demo #1 to compare against. One asked you to spend time next round recapping where you started. The second is real and worth sitting with: your relevance judgments are still graded by your own team. One reviewer put it precisely — "relevance judgments still relied on the team's own assessment" — and another said they remained "confused about what success criteria and how you are measuring relevance."
On the credit side, you did something no other team did: you compared against a keyword-search baseline. That is the right move, because it answers "is the agent better than the obvious cheap alternative?" rather than just "does the agent work?" Build on it for Demo #3 by making the relevance judgments independent — have someone outside the team label the same results, and report how often you two disagree before you report the agent's score. One more note from several reviewers: the presentation ran long, the filter UI was hard to read, and the agent's font was too small to see from the back. Also, one reviewer couldn't follow how the bidding work connects to RFIs — worth one slide.
